Nitrogen fixation rates increase with diazotroph richness in the global ocean

Eriksson, D., et al. | Scientific Reports

DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-61132-2

Core Question

  • The Diversity-Function Gap: Investigates whether a positive Biodiversity-Ecosystem Functioning (BEF) relationship exists between marine microbial diversity and global nutrient cycles.
  • Global Scale Assessment: Examines how the species richness of marine diazotrophs impacts global ocean nitrogen fixation rates.

Key Methodology

  • Data Integration: Combined traditional microscopy occurrence records with advanced molecular data (qPCR and metagenomics).
  • Ensemble Modeling: Employed 90 ensemble Species Distribution Models (SDMs) utilizing GLMs, GAMs, and Random Forests.

Principal Findings

  • Robust BEF Coupling: Total diazotroph richness strongly correlates with global marine nitrogen fixation rates (median Spearman's ρ ≥ 0.80).
  • Latitudinal Diversity Gradient: Diazotroph species richness peaks cleanly within the tropics and drops by half toward polar boundaries.

Broader Implications

  • Macroecological Validation: Confirms positive BEF relationships are a persistent, emergent property of plankton communities globally.
  • Ecosystem Buffering: Maintaining a diverse pool of metabolic strategies provides a critical functional buffer for global marine processes.

Variations in the latitudinal diversity gradients of the ocean microbiome

Eriksson, D., Schiller, J., Schickele, A., et al. | Cell Host & Microbe

DOI: 10.1016/j.chom.2026.05.016

Core Question

  • Gradient Structural Deviations: Investigating if structural variations break the classical symmetric Latitudinal Diversity Gradient across specialized bacterial domains.
  • Eco-Niche Segmentation: Assessing how lifestyle configurations (free-living vs. particle-attached layers) scale structurally across latitudes.

Key Methodology

  • Genomic Sequencing Triggers: Aggregated macroecological filters leveraging broad global ocean metagenomic and metatranscriptomic data assets.
  • Spatial Depth Deconstruction: Evaluated layer breakdowns explicitly separating epipelagic horizons from mesopelagic boundary tracks.

Principal Findings

  • Asymmetric Gradient Anomalies: Discovered localized asymmetries where traditional macroecological diversity rules vary significantly across microclimates and depths.
  • Lifestyle Divergence: Free-living bacteria follow clear temperature dependencies, while particle-attached profiles correlate heavily with organic carbon export vectors.

Broader Implications

  • Macroecological Modeling Corrections: Proves that uniform spatial projections fail to capture microbial variations, requiring depth-stratified parameters.
  • Climatic Vulnerability Mapping: Indicates that climate transitions will cause non-uniform structural shifts across changing global ocean layers.
