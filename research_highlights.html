Nitrogen fixation rates increase with diazotroph richness in the global ocean

Eriksson, D., et al. (2026) | Scientific Reports

DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-61132-2

Core Methodology

Integrated global datasets of marine observation measurements with advanced empirical modeling frameworks to accurately trace the direct correlation between microbial richness metrics and ecosystem function kinetics.

Key Results & Implications

Proved that higher species richness among diazotroph networks directly boosts total ocean nitrogen fixation rates, highlighting the foundational role of preserving microbial diversity amid shifting global climates.

Variations in the latitudinal diversity gradients of the ocean microbiome

Eriksson, D., Schiller, J., Schickele, A., et al. (2026) | Cell Host & Microbe

DOI: 10.1016/j.chom.2026.05.016

Core Methodology

Utilized extensive genomic and meta-transcriptomic sequencing filters alongside macroecological spatial models to evaluate community distribution across latitudinal lines.

Key Results & Implications

Uncovered unexpected, non-linear structural anomalies in the latitudinal diversity gradients of the ocean microbiome, showing that traditional macroecological diversity rules vary significantly across microclimates.
